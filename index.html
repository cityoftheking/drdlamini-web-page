About

A career built on the discipline of optimism.

I was born and raised in Zimbabwe during some of the country's most challenging economic years. That experience taught me resilience, optimism and the value of finding agency even when circumstances are difficult.

I am proud of that heritage and journey. It informs how I lead and my belief that much of what we assume is impossible can become possible. My career has crossed finance, private-sector management, public service, organizational leadership, governance, applied research and entrepreneurship.

I have served on the board of a community credit union and as a trustee in one of the world's largest public retirement systems. I completed a Doctorate in Leadership and Innovation at the University of the Pacific.

The speaking and research shared here reflect my personal views and independent work. They do not represent the position of any employer or agency I serve.

Meeting of the Minds 2026 · Monterey, California

Empowering Those Who Power Organizations: AI Adoption Starts With People

In September 2026, I delivered the closing keynote at the California Workforce Association's Meeting of the Minds conference. The keynote focused on a simple proposition: AI adoption is not only a technology challenge. It is an opportunity to increase agency, learning and human capability across organizations.

The session connected research on empowerment with the realities leaders and practitioners face as AI changes work. It encouraged organizations to make adoption something people help shape rather than something that simply happens to them.

Research

When AI adoption starts with people, the story changes.

My doctoral research used participatory action research to examine how public-sector professionals could explore generative AI through training, structured experimentation and shared learning. The people doing the work were co-researchers who helped shape how the technology could serve their work.

11co-researchers participated in the study
8 of 9respondents after the study (89%) reported feeling more empowered
7 of 9respondents at the three-month follow-up (78%) reported daily or weekly AI use

Findings from a small participatory study, not a controlled evaluation. Explore the survey data ↗

Governor's Innovation Fellows · September 2026

Bringing research into a conversation about public-sector innovation.

In September 2026, I spoke with Cohort 3 of the Governor's Innovation Fellows and shared an overview of my dissertation study on generative AI, empowerment and program administration. This was the second Governor's Innovation Fellows cohort I presented to in the past year, following a session with Cohort 2.

The conversation focused on what the research suggests about human-centered adoption: build baseline capability, create room for thoughtful experimentation, and give the people closest to the work meaningful agency in shaping how new tools are used.

Two cohorts · One recurring question

How do public organizations adopt emerging technology in a way that strengthens, rather than diminishes, the agency of the people doing the work?
