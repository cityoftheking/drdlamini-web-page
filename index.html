The work

I build the connective tissue complex organizations need.

My career has moved across finance, management, public policy, governance, research, and organizational innovation. The common thread is straightforward: bring the right people together, make the problem clear, and build a path from intent to implementation.

01

Lead complex systems

Stand up new functions, move resources through complicated delivery systems, and build operating structures that hold across independent organizations.

02

Build human-centered innovation

Use evidence, experimentation and the expertise closest to the work to adopt new tools without losing judgment, agency or mission.

03

Convene across boundaries

Create spaces where executives, practitioners, public agencies, advocates and partners can move from different interests toward shared direction.

Bulawayo to California: the through-line is optimism, learning and leaving things better than I found them.

A wider lens

Experience across systems changes how you see the problem.

I was born and raised in Zimbabwe and came of age during a period of profound economic disruption. I watched people around me keep making a plan, keep learning, and keep moving even when circumstances offered very little certainty.

That experience still shapes how I lead. Optimism, to me, is not pretending a problem is smaller than it is. It is the discipline of believing there is something useful we can do next—and then doing the work to find it.

Research spotlight

When AI adoption starts with people, the story changes.

My doctoral research used participatory action research to examine how public-sector professionals could explore generative AI through training, structured experimentation and shared learning. The result was not a technology-first rollout. It was a study of agency, capability and how people closest to the work can shape adoption.
