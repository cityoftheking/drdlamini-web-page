Research

Generative AI is a people story.

My doctoral research examined how generative AI could empower program staff working in a large public institution—not by treating them as end-users of a rollout, but as co-researchers capable of shaping how the technology could serve their work.

Dissertation

Empowering State-Level Multilingual Education Leaders: Harnessing Generative Artificial Intelligence for Program Administration

A qualitative participatory action research study involving 11 members of a state-level multilingual education division. Co-researchers received approved training, joined a community of practice, developed use cases, experimented with tools and reflected together on what changed.

11co-researchers
89%reported feeling more empowered
78%continued using AI at three-month follow-up

What the study tested

What happens when people help design adoption?

The study centered empowerment theory and participatory action research. That means the people doing the work were not merely subjects. They helped identify problems, test use cases, interpret experience and share what they learned.

TRAIN

Build baseline capability

Give people enough understanding to explore responsibly instead of asking them to improvise from uncertainty.

EXPLORE

Start with real work

Use domain expertise to identify tasks where AI might improve effectiveness, support judgment or expand capacity.

REFLECT

Learn in community

Share results, failures, concerns and emerging practices so the organization learns faster than individuals can alone.

Key insight

Assisted capability is not the same as human capability.

AI can help people produce more, faster. That is useful—but output is not the whole story. Organizations also need to ask what is happening to confidence, judgment, learning, ownership and decision authority as work changes.

This is why I focus on empowerment rather than adoption alone. The stronger question is not simply, “Are people using AI?” It is, “Are people becoming more capable of shaping how AI serves the mission?”

THE QUESTION

How can generative AI empower program staff in managing state-level multilingual programs?

The study found evidence of psychological and structural empowerment, alongside barriers related to technology, platform constraints and individual preparedness.
